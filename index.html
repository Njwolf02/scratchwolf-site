What the state lottery publishes, made useful

01

Prize counts, refreshed daily

State lotteries disclose how many top prizes remain on each game. We collect those numbers every morning, so you can skip a ticket whose jackpots were all claimed months ago.

02

One score per game

Odds, prize pool, expected value, and trend, combined into a single 0–100 Buy Score. A $5 game in Kentucky is compared against other Kentucky games, because that's where you're actually standing in line — the best of them each morning is your Wolf Pick.

03

The "skip these" list, free

Games with dead prize pools get flagged for everyone, no subscription required. A wolf that chases every deer starves — knowing what not to buy is half the point.

04

Alerts when things change

A top prize gets claimed, a game starts selling out, a fresh game launches with a full pool. You'll hear about it the same morning we do — including when a game's Wolf Factor crosses 1.5x, meaning its jackpots are outlasting its ticket supply.

05

AI Watch, and what it actually does

A model estimates how likely each game is to lose a top prize in the next fortnight, and flags the ones closest to it. It is scored every night on data it has never seen — 0.92 AUC on 187,000 held-out observations — and it covers all 44 states, including the ones that publish too little to compute a ticket's value at all. It tells you which games to act on before the prize is gone, or skip. It does not predict a winning ticket, and nothing can.

A data tool, not a gambling service

ScratchWolf doesn't sell tickets, take wagers, or award prizes. We analyze the public data that state lotteries are required to publish. Every lottery game loses money on average. We show you that math too. And when the smartest play is not to play, the app will tell you.

Common questions about scratch-off odds

Which scratch-off ticket has the best odds?

It depends on your state, and the answer changes as prizes are claimed. We rank the shortest published overall odds in every state, updated every morning — see scratch-offs with the best odds in America. But the game with the best printed odds is usually the one that wins most often, not the one worth the most: overall odds count any prize, including a prize equal to the ticket price.

Do better overall odds mean a better ticket?

No, and we measured it. Comparing eight ranking rules over 90 days of daily prize counts, ranking by best overall odds came out worst — the highest collapse rate of any rule at 1.86%, against 1.47% for ranking on expected value. Nearly every scratch-off site ranks on that one number anyway.

How do I know if a scratch-off still has top prizes left?

Every state lottery publishes how many prizes remain in each game, but rarely in a form you can compare. We recount all of it each morning and show what is actually still unclaimed, so you can avoid buying into a game whose big prizes are already gone.

Do more expensive tickets have better odds?

Usually yes — higher price tiers carry shorter overall odds and higher payout percentages, and that held up in our own testing. It is still not the strongest signal: how much of the prize pool is left matters more than the price on the front of the ticket.

How often is this updated?

Every morning. We re-read each state lottery's own published prize tables daily, so the remaining-prize counts reflect the most recent figures the state has released. The printed overall odds never change — they are fixed when a game goes to print.
